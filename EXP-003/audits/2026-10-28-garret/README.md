## Scope and preregistration

The [EXP-003 protocol](../../protocol.md) freezes the two original monitors, separate threshold calibration, seed splits, endpoints and project-screen criteria. It does **not** preregister Garret's full 2x2 control or variance-fault extension.

The supplied README calls the absolute/.97 arm “preregistered A2.” The precise qualification is that its operation corresponds to A2, “Destroy sign information,” in the repository-root [ABLATIONS.csv](../../../ABLATIONS.csv), part of the EXP-001 design packet. That earlier listing predicts the lead advantage will shrink toward baseline when absolute residuals replace signed residuals inside the EFMW EWMA. It is not an EXP-003-specific preregistration of this four-arm analysis. The original attachment is retained unchanged; this paragraph clarifies its scope rather than rewriting it.

The variance test is explicitly identified by Garret as new and not preregistered. It uses the same degradation seed range and original per-arm null-calibrated thresholds, without recalibration to the new fault.

## Results

All counts are out of 80. “Gain” is the median of **paired per-seed differences**, not the difference between marginal medians. Misses enter all-run lead calculations as -999, following the frozen protocol.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| Absolute, decay .90 (baseline) | 65 | 3 | 12 | 16.5 | 0 | 0 / 0; 1 |
| Absolute, decay .97 | 74 | 1 | 5 | 25.0 | +2.0 [0.5, 5.0] | 10 / 1; .01171875 |
| Signed, decay .90 | 80 | 0 | 0 | 53.5 | +33.5 [28.5, 41.0] | 15 / 0; .0000610352 |
| Signed, decay .97 (EFMW) | 77 | 3 | 0 | 59.0 | +41.5 [37.0, 47.0] | 15 / 3; .00753784 |

The original EFMW/baseline comparison meets the frozen project screen: gain at least 10 steps; held-out false-alarm incidence at most 10% for each; EFMW actionable detection at least 60%. The data support the original reported pass on this synthetic setting.

### Garret's exploratory variance-fault extension

| Arm | Actionable | Pre-onset | At/after failure | Median lead, all runs | Paired gain vs baseline [95% bootstrap CI] | McNemar arm-only / baseline-only; p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| Absolute, decay .90 (baseline) | 77 | 3 | 0 | 58.5 | 0 | 0 / 0; 1 |
| Absolute, decay .97 | 79 | 1 | 0 | 60.5 | 0.0 [0.0, 1.5] | 3 / 1; .625 |
| Signed, decay .90 | 47 | 0 | 33 | 10.0 | -50.5 [-1017, -34] | 2 / 32; 6.94e-8 |

Every degradation trace eventually has an alert; the failures are pre-onset or too-late alerts, not absent alerts. Among seeds on which both the EFMW and baseline arms give actionable warnings, EFMW's median paired gain remains -24 steps (n=40). The reversed ranking is therefore not solely produced by assigning -999 to misses, although the very large negative confidence-limit values are consequences of that convention, not literal thousand-step delays.

## Interpretation and limitations

1. **Signed averaging matters in the mean-drift setting.** At decay .90, switching to signed averaging gives a +33.5 median paired gain; changing only the absolute arm's decay gives +2.0. This supports a substantial role for signed persistence, rather than attributing the entire original advantage to longer memory. It is not a formal additive decomposition, and these medians cannot be subtracted to estimate independent causal contributions.
2. **The fault class matters.** The patch removes the additive bias and sinusoid and multiplies zero-mean process noise by `(1 + 1.5*d)`. It retains `a = .82 + .08*d`, the onset/failure convention and random-draw sequence. “Zero-mean variance fault” describes the introduced noise; the intervention is not a pure variance-only change in every system property because the autoregressive coefficient still changes with degradation.
3. **The extension is exploratory.** Its ranking reversal limits broad claims of robustness but does not falsify the frozen original comparison. Eighty synthetic seeds and one added fault setting do not establish real-world deployment performance or universal superiority of either monitor. Multiple reported contrasts are not adjusted for multiplicity.
4. **Failure is a simulator-defined endpoint.** Actionability is measured against the specified onset/failure times, not demonstrated physical equipment failure or operator benefit.
5. **The miss convention matters.** Report detection counts alongside all-run lead statistics and the both-detected sensitivity check. The latter is conditioned on joint success and is not a replacement primary endpoint.
6. **No new physical theory is established.** The comparisons concern explicit residual/EWMA operations in the stated generator. A successful synthetic screen is not evidence for broader theoretical, physical or field-validity claims.

## Provenance and reproduction caveats

Garret's logs identify source commit `8f11bc5320cffb10932d5d31da7072d6ad4f65c2`, Python 3.11.18, NumPy 2.4.6, an exact container digest, CPU-only execution and no network during the run. They report EXP-003 source equality to commits `65134993069c2dc0c46a60fccd8bcdb2d951cdd3` and `aa7cc9183220df1eb39537107f38cbce8f51e515`. They also report identical patched default/null paths and generic-arm agreement with the published monitors to a maximum absolute difference of about 4.16e-17. These execution claims are attributed to the supplied logs, not rerun for this archive.

The repository's original protocol, monitor definitions, A2 listing and current source layout were inspected at the stated source commit. The supplied logs explain the original calibration/results CSV hash discrepancy as LF versus CRLF only and record normalized-byte comparisons. This review preserves the evidence without treating a log of a comparison as a newly performed end-to-end test.

The driver patches a copy of the simulator, not the published source. However, invoking the original `run.py` rewrites `EXP-003/calibration.csv` and `EXP-003/results.csv`; therefore the supplied README's broad wording about files never being modified must not be read as promising a write-free working tree. Use a disposable checkout for a future rerun. The runner uses `set -u`, not `set -e`, and logs subprocess exits rather than stopping on every failure; check those exit records explicitly. The supplied logs show exit 0 for the two principal runs.

For a future authorized full rerun, use the original package README's environment and source revision and copy the archived `.py.txt`, `.sh.txt` and `.patch.txt` files to their original executable/patch names in a separate checkout. The archive intentionally leaves attachment names and bytes unchanged and does not execute them automatically.
